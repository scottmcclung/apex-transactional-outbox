## Benchmark

Commands: `node bench.js bench_noop 1,10,100 120` once, then `node bench.js bench_noop 100 120` four more times, so there are five samples at 100 messages. The samples were run one after another, not at the same time. The first 100-message sample is the one printed by the sweep.

"Run CPU" and "run heap" are the maximums printed by `bench.js` as seen at each send. The main rows are from `2026-11-17-resolver-messages.md`.

At 100 messages, the median run CPU over five samples is 42 ms, against 39 ms for main. The 4-1 samples range from 38 to 44 ms. The main samples range from 37 to 43 ms. The ranges overlap, but the median is 3 ms higher. This is not confirmed as a difference. A fair comparison needs main and this branch measured in the same session, which means redeploying main to the org. That was not done for this file.

Run heap is 3 bytes higher at 1, 10 and 100 messages, against main. The offset is the same at every size, and it was identical in all five 100-message samples. The intended change does not account for it, and its cause is not yet known.

Each outbox completed with one attempt in every run. Publish-side and run-side query, DML and relay job counts match main at every size.

Delay from publish to completion was 14 seconds for the single-message run, and 1 second at the median for 10 and 100 messages. The main file recorded between 0 and 1 second. Delay is not compared here.
